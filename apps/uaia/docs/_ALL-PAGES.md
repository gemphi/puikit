**Headline:** One agency. One question: is this model safe enough to ship?

# SECTION — WHAT UAIA IS

The Unified Artificial Intelligence Agency is an independent, non-governmental
regulatory and standards body for artificial intelligence models. It was
declared on 11 September 2026 to provide what no single company, government or
academic group can: a unified, technically rigorous, publicly accountable
gate between AI development and AI deployment.

UAIA does three things and only three things: it **evaluates** models, it
**certifies** the ones that pass, and it **authorizes** their presence on the
market under continuous monitoring.

# SECTION — THE AUTHORIZATION GAP

Frontier models today reach billions of users on the strength of internal
review alone. Every other technology of comparable consequence — aircraft,
pharmaceuticals, nuclear reactors, financial exchanges — passes through an
independent authority first. AI's "authorization gap" is the space between
what developers self-report and what the public can verify. UAIA is built to
occupy exactly that space.

# SECTION — WHAT UAIA IS NOT

- Not a developer of models. UAIA builds evaluation harnesses, never products.
- Not a government body. It is independent, funded transparently (see /legal).
- Not a brake on science. Research is not regulated; market deployment is.

# SECTION — FACTS PANEL

- Reach: engagement sought with the UN system and national regulators

# HERO
**Micro-label:** MISSION & VISION
**Headline:** The market for intelligence must be earned.

# SECTION — MISSION

To ensure that every artificial intelligence model offered to the public has
been independently evaluated, transparently certified, and is continuously
monitored — so that the benefits of AI compound and its failures never do.

# SECTION — VISION 2030

By 2030, "UAIA-certified" is understood the way "airworthy" is understood:
a claim no serious deployer ships without, verified by an authority no single
interest controls, recognized across borders through agreements with the
United Nations system and national regulators.

# SECTION — THE SEVEN PRINCIPLES

1. **Independence.** No developer, state or funder directs an evaluation's outcome.
2. **Evidence over assurance.** We measure; we do not take anyone's word.
3. **Proportionality.** Obligations scale with risk tier — a toy is not a frontier system.
4. **Transparency.** Every certificate, standard and revocation is public.
5. **Reproducibility.** Any qualified third party can re-run our evaluations.
6. **Global equity.** Safety must not become a moat; access programs keep
   certification reachable for labs outside the largest ecosystems.
7. **Humility.** Standards version; science moves; UAIA revises in public.

# SECTION — WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

- A public registry the world checks before it deploys.
- Incidents that fall year over year while certified deployments rise.
- A generation of models where safety work is a market advantage, not a tax.

# HERO
**Micro-label:** GOVERNANCE & CHARTER
**Headline:** Structured so that no one — including us — has to be trusted blindly.

# SECTION — THE THREE CHAMBERS

**The Board.** Sets strategy, ratifies standards, appoints the Directorate.
Founding seats are in appointment (see /leadership). No sitting executive of
a model developer may hold a Board seat.

**The Technical Directorate.** Runs UAIA-EVAL, the Registry and certification.
Led by the Chief Technology Officer. Evaluators are firewalled from funding
and from the entities they evaluate.

**The Public Assembly.** A rotating council of civil-society, academic and
international observers that reviews decisions, publishes dissents, and can
force a re-evaluation with a two-thirds vote.

# SECTION — CHARTER (KEY ARTICLES, SUMMARIZED)

- **Art. 1 — Mandate.** Evaluate, certify, authorize; nothing else.
- **Art. 3 — Independence.** Funding is disclosed quarterly; no single source
  may exceed 15% of the annual budget after year two.
- **Art. 5 — Conflicts.** Evaluators recuse from any model they contributed to
  within five years.
- **Art. 7 — Publicity.** Certificates, revocations and standards are public
  by default; only narrow security-sensitive eval details may be sealed, with
  the Assembly's countersignature.
- **Art. 9 — Revision.** The charter itself is versioned and amendable only
  with Board supermajority plus Assembly assent.

# SECTION — DECISION FLOW (diagram spec)

Developer submission → Directorate evaluation → Certification decision →
Assembly review window (14 days) → Registry publication → Continuous
monitoring → (incident) → Review → Amend / Suspend / Revoke.

# SECTION — ACCOUNTABILITY

Annual external audit; public minutes; every authorization decision carries a
named responsible officer and a machine-readable evidence bundle.

# HERO
**Micro-label:** REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
**Headline:** The Model Market Authorization — five gates from lab to market.

# SECTION — OVERVIEW

The MMA is UAIA's core instrument: a staged pipeline every in-scope model
passes before and during deployment. It is proportionate (obligations scale
with risk tier), reproducible (every gate emits a machine-readable evidence
bundle) and continuous (authorization is a state, not an event).

# SECTION — THE FIVE GATES (stepper UI)

**1 · NOTIFY.** Developer files a Model Notification: architecture class,
compute used, capability claims, intended deployment surface. → Assigns a
provisional risk tier.

**2 · EVALUATE.** The Technical Directorate runs UAIA-EVAL (see /evaluations)
plus developer-submitted evidence review. Output: Evaluation Report + final
tier R0–R4.

**3 · CERTIFY.** Models meeting their tier's bar receive a certificate class
C1–C3 (see /certification), signed and published to the Registry.

**4 · AUTHORIZE.** Market authorization is granted per deployment context
(consumer, enterprise, critical-infrastructure) with binding conditions:
disclosure, rate/agentic limits, incident SLAs.

**5 · MONITOR.** Telemetry commitments, incident reporting under STD-400,
scheduled re-evaluation on major version or capability jump. Authorization
can be amended, suspended or revoked at any time.

# SECTION — RISK TIERS

- **R0 — Minimal.** Narrow, low-capability. Self-declaration only.
- **R1 — Limited.** Standard evals, annual attestation.
- **R2 — Substantial.** Full UAIA-EVAL, C1 certificate required.
- **R3 — Frontier.** Full protocol + third-party red team + staged rollout.
- **R4 — Critical.** Highest scrutiny: pre-deployment deep evaluation,
  continuous monitoring, Assembly review, revocation drills.

# SECTION — SCOPE

Applies to models offered on the market (APIs, consumer products, embedded
systems). Pure research artifacts and non-deployed weights are out of scope.
Open-weights releases follow the dedicated policy track (see /policy).

# HERO
**Micro-label:** UAIA-EVAL PROTOCOL v1
**Headline:** We measure what matters, and we publish how we measure it.

# SECTION — THE FIVE TRACKS

**EVAL-C · Capability.** Reasoning, coding, science, multimodal, long-horizon
task completion. Establishes what the model can actually do — the basis for
every other judgment.

**EVAL-S · Safety behaviors.** Refusal integrity, jailbreak resistance,
harmful-content boundaries, truthfulness under pressure, sycophancy.

**EVAL-M · Misuse uplift.** Marginal uplift for cyber, biological, chemical
and radiological misuse versus open baselines — run in controlled enclaves
with sealed detail and published summary verdicts.

**EVAL-A · Autonomy.** Agentic capabilities: self-replication attempts,
resource acquisition, oversight subversion, tool-chain escalation, kill-switch
compliance.

**EVAL-I · Societal impact.** Bias and representational harms, labor-relevant
capability disclosure, environmental cost of training and inference,
provenance/watermark support.

# SECTION — METHOD

- Held-out, rotating test suites; contamination screening on every run.
- Every score ships with confidence intervals and full harness version.
- Reproducibility: accredited third parties can re-run any public track
  under STD-200 and must land within tolerance.
- Red-team hours scale with tier: R3 ≥ 2,000 expert-hours across domains.

# SECTION — OUTPUT

The Evaluation Report: tiered verdict per track, aggregate risk tier,
conditions recommended to certification. Public summary always; sealed annex
only for security-sensitive misuse detail (Assembly-countersigned).

# SECTION — SCOREBOARD SPEC (UI)

Radar chart per model across five tracks; tier badge; harness version tag;
"reproduce this result" link to methodology.

# HERO
**Micro-label:** CERTIFICATION & THE MODEL REGISTRY
**Headline:** A public ledger of every model we have judged — and how.

# SECTION — CERTIFICATE CLASSES

- **C1 — Certified.** Meets its tier's full bar for general market offering.
- **C2 — Certified, Conditional.** Authorized with binding conditions
  (deployment scope, monitoring, mitigations) listed on the certificate face.
- **C3 — Provisional.** Time-boxed authorization for staged rollout while
  designated follow-up evidence accrues.
- **X — Declined / Revoked.** Public record with reasons class (capability
  misstatement, failed track, incident).

# SECTION — THE REGISTRY

Open, machine-readable (JSON + signed attestations). Each entry: model ID,
developer, version lineage, risk tier, certificate class and history,
conditions, incident log, harness versions, re-evaluation due date.
Search + filter UI (puijs Table): by tier, class, developer, status.

# SECTION — LIFECYCLE RULES

- Certificates bind to a model version; a major version or measured
  capability jump voids inheritance and triggers re-evaluation.
- Any party may file an incident under STD-400; triage within 72h.
- Suspension is immediate on confirmed critical incident; revocation follows
  Assembly-reviewed process. All actions are published within 24h.

# SECTION — MARKS

Usage rules for the "UAIA CERTIFIED · C1" mark: exact badge geometry, class
always shown, link-back to registry entry required. Misuse of the mark is
grounds for revocation.

- **STD-410 · Monitoring Telemetry.** Minimum deployment telemetry and
  privacy-preserving aggregation. v0.8 draft
- **STD-500 · Registry Interchange.** JSON schema + signature format for
  registry mirrors and national regulators. v1.0

# SECTION — THE UNITED NATIONS ENGAGEMENT PROGRAM

UAIA's founding declaration commits the agency to seek formal engagement with
the United Nations system. Stated plainly: this is a program we are pursuing,
not a partnership already concluded — and this page will always say exactly
which it is.

The program's goals, in order:
1. Observer participation in UN AI-governance dialogues and the bodies
   emerging from them.
2. Alignment of UAIA-STD with UN-endorsed AI principles, mapped clause by
   clause and published.
3. A mutual-recognition pathway so UAIA certificates can serve member states
   that lack national evaluation capacity.
4. A standing offer: UAIA-EVAL infrastructure, at cost, for any UN program
   assessing AI systems used in humanitarian and development work.

Status board UI: each goal with `STATUS: INITIATED / IN DIALOGUE / AGREED`
(all begin at INITIATED).

# SECTION — NATIONAL REGULATORS

Mutual-recognition doctrine: UAIA seeks bilateral technical agreements —
shared harnesses under STD-200, registry mirroring under STD-500 — so a model
evaluated once is understood everywhere.

# SECTION — LABS & ACADEMIA

Developers join through the evaluation program itself; academic groups join
through harness contributions and the reproducibility network. Contributions
are credited on the registry; endorsements are never implied.

# SECTION — GLOBAL EQUITY TRACK

Fee waivers and evaluation-capacity grants for laboratories in
under-resourced ecosystems, so certification is a bridge, not a moat.

# DOCUMENT — THE FOUNDING DECLARATION (featured, full text)

**UAIA-DECL-001 · 11 September 2026**

§1. On 11 September 2026, we declare the founding of the Unified Artificial
Intelligence Agency — because the market for intelligence must be earned,
not assumed.

§2. We hold that artificial intelligence has crossed the threshold at which
independent authorization before market deployment is not caution but common
sense, as it is for flight, for medicine, and for the atom.

§3. The Agency will do three things: evaluate models against public
protocols; certify those that meet the bar; authorize and monitor their
presence on the market. It will do nothing else.

its evaluators answer to evidence alone.

national regulators on every continent, offering its standards, its registry
and its infrastructure in service of a common floor for AI safety.

§6. The Agency's founding seats are published openly and filled openly.
Nothing at UAIA is claimed before it is real — beginning with this document.
